\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Large scale plant identification has been organised around the LifeCLEF evaluation campaigns~\cite{lifeclef2026}. Early systems relied on hand crafted features such as leaf shape descriptors and colour histograms~\cite{goeau2013pl}. Deep convolutional networks then brought substantial gains on single specimen classification~\cite{lee2017deep}, and recent work has shifted to Vision Transformers, which capture long range spatial structure within images~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image}. The 2024 and 2025 editions of PlantCLEF introduced the vegetation plot setting and with it the gap between specimen level training data and plot level test data~\cite{goeau2024plantclef}. The strongest systems in those editions bridged the gap with tiled inference in the style of SAHI slicing, test time augmentation, and ensembling.

Several pretrained vision models are relevant to this work. BioCLIP~\cite{stevens2024bioclip} is a contrastive vision language model trained on TreeOfLife-10M, whose hierarchical taxonomic captions align its feature space with the Linnaean taxonomy and outperform ImageNet pretrained features for species identification. DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} is a self supervised Vision Transformer with strong cross domain transfer and spatial correspondence, and ConvNeXt-V2~\cite{woo2023convnextv2} pairs a modernised convolutional architecture with masked autoencoder pretraining. We use all three as frozen feature extractors in the late fusion baseline of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Fully fine tuning such models is often counterproductive on noisy, long tailed data, since it risks catastrophic forgetting and overfitting; a common alternative freezes most of the backbone and adapts only the top transformer blocks, which is especially favourable for a taxonomically aligned model whose pretraining prior is what makes it valuable.

For multi label and long tailed settings, Asymmetric Loss~\cite{ridnik2021asymmetric} extends focal loss~\cite{lin2017focal} by down weighting the easy negatives that dominate when there are thousands of classes, and logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021longtail} shifts logits by class prior probabilities so that common classes must produce larger margins than rare ones. Our final system applies prior based logit adjustment at inference time.
