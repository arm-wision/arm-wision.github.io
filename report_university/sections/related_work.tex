\chapter{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Large-scale plant identification has been organised around the LifeCLEF evaluation campaigns~\cite{lifeclef2026}. Early systems relied on hand-crafted features such as leaf-shape descriptors and colour histograms~\cite{goeau2013pl}. Deep convolutional networks then brought substantial gains on single-specimen classification~\cite{lee2017deep}, and recent work has shifted to Vision Transformers, which capture long-range spatial structure within images~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image}. The 2024 edition of PlantCLEF introduced the vegetation-plot setting and with it the gap between specimen-level training data and plot-level test data~\cite{goeau2024plantclef}. The strongest systems in that setting bridged the gap with sliced tiled inference, test-time augmentation, and ensembling.

Several pretrained vision models are relevant to this work. The original BioCLIP~\cite{stevens2024bioclip} is a contrastive vision-language model trained on TreeOfLife-10M; its hierarchical taxonomic captions align the learned features with the Linnaean taxonomy, and those features outperform ImageNet-pretrained features for species identification. BioCLIP~2~\cite{gu2025bioclip2} scales this recipe to the larger TreeOfLife-200M dataset. The specific model we use is the BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 checkpoint released by the same group~\cite{bioclip25hf}, which applies the BioCLIP~2 training recipe to a ViT-H/14 backbone on an expanded TreeOfLife-200M corpus, loaded from the Hugging Face Hub as \texttt{imageomics/bioclip-2.5-vith14}. DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} is a self-supervised Vision Transformer with strong cross-domain transfer and spatial correspondence, and ConvNeXt-V2~\cite{woo2023convnextv2} pairs a modernised convolutional architecture with masked-autoencoder pretraining. We use all three as frozen feature extractors in the late-fusion baseline of Section~\ref{sec:experiments}. Fully fine-tuning such models is often counterproductive on noisy, long-tailed data, since it risks catastrophic forgetting and overfitting; a common alternative freezes most of the backbone and adapts only the top transformer blocks, which is especially favourable for a taxonomically aligned model whose pretrained representation is what makes it valuable.

Long-tailed classification has motivated several loss functions. Asymmetric Loss~\cite{ridnik2021asymmetric} extends focal loss~\cite{lin2017focal} by down-weighting easy negatives; we note it as background but do not use it, since our setting is single-label at training time. Logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021longtail} instead shifts logits by class-prior probabilities so that common classes must produce larger margins than rare ones, and our final system applies this prior-based adjustment at inference time.
