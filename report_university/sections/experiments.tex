\chapter{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We justify each component of the final system with leaderboard evidence. Many comparisons
move more than one factor at once; we flag the confounds where they occur and treat this
as a development trace rather than a single controlled ablation.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}

The task is scored by the macro-averaged per-quadrat F1 of \S\ref{sec:introduction} on a
hidden test set of $2{,}105$ vegetation quadrats. Kaggle reports a \emph{public} score on
a fixed subset of quadrats and a \emph{private} score on the complement; the two do not
always rank configurations the same way (\S\ref{sec:infer_ablations}), so we report both
wherever a configuration was submitted. Repeated near-identical submissions put the
run-to-run leaderboard noise floor at $\approx 0.002$ Macro F1, and we treat smaller
differences as not significant. We also track held-out top-1/top-5 accuracy on a $10\%$
single-plant validation split, but it correlates only weakly with quadrat Macro F1
($r\approx0.4$; \S\ref{sec:val_lb}), so we use it to choose checkpoints, not to rank
submissions.

\section{Baselines}

Off-the-shelf BioCLIP features go only so far on this task: zero-shot tile classification
reaches ${\sim}0.12$ public Macro F1, a frozen-backbone linear probe $0.242$, a few-shot
support bank $0.307$, and a single-head partial fine-tune $0.222$. BioCLIP carries useful
biological signal, but neither zero-shot matching nor a frozen backbone bridges the
single-plant\,$\to$\,multi-species shift; we carry only the fine-tuning line forward.

\section{Supervised Fine-tuning}

Partially fine-tuning BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 with the taxonomy-aware head gives the decisive
gains. The first strong model (experiment~010: shared trunk, five levels, $1.41$M images)
reached $0.391$ public Macro F1. Our final model (experiment~i002: per-head MLPs, three
levels, $2.65$M images, ten epochs) reached $0.41165$ public / $0.38132$ private at
single-resolution inference, before calibration and ensembling. The $010\rightarrow$i002
comparison changes training data, schedule, head topology and head count simultaneously,
so we report i002 as the stronger configuration without isolating any single cause.

\section{Backbone Unfreezing}
\label{sec:blocks_ablation}

The one factor we swept within a single experiment is the number of unfrozen transformer
blocks. Within experiment~010 (warm-started stages, five epochs each) public Macro F1
rises from the head-only baseline ($0.362$) to a peak at \emph{eight} blocks ($0.391$) and
declines at twelve; the private split is flatter (four/eight/twelve within $0.006$), and
the full fine-tune collapses to $0.301$ public, trading the pretrained representation for
single-plant fit faster than the leaderboard rewards (Table~\ref{tab:blocks}). Validation
top-5 keeps rising with depth even as the leaderboard score peaks and falls --- the
decoupling of \S\ref{sec:val_lb}.

\section{Tile Aggregation}
\label{sec:tile_aggregation}

Of the three pooling rules defined in \S\ref{sec:tile_agg_method}, softmax-mean wins. On a
matched head-only 010 checkpoint, plain mean was clearly weakest ($0.258$ public against
$0.338$ for max and $0.340$ for softmax-mean), consistent with its dilution of few-tile
evidence, so we dropped it. On the i002 four-block checkpoint the submitted comparison was
max against softmax-mean: softmax-mean won on both splits ($0.41165$ vs.\ $0.37509$ public;
$0.38883$ vs.\ $0.35724$ private). We adopt softmax-mean with the adaptive probability
threshold for the final system.

\section{Calibration and Ensembling}
\label{sec:infer_ablations}

On the i002 four-block checkpoint we fixed the remaining inference knobs by leaderboard
sweep (public Macro F1, single-resolution $224$-pixel unless noted).

\textbf{Logit adjustment $\tau$ and threshold $T$.} A joint sweep over
$\tau\in\{0.25,0.5\}$ and $T\in\{0.03,0.05,0.07\}$ has a sharp optimum at $\tau{=}0.25$,
$T{=}0.03$, which dominates every other cell (Table~\ref{tab:tau_T}); Macro F1 falls off
monotonically along both axes, and doubling $\tau$ at fixed $T$ already costs $0.022$. These
two values were selected by following our public-leaderboard score progression rather than on a
held-out multi-species validation set, which we lacked: they are a leaderboard-guided operating
point chosen as a reasonable balance under a limited submission budget, not the product of a
rigorous hyperparameter search, which we leave to future work (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\textbf{Resolution ensemble.} Averaging the same checkpoint at $224$ and $336$ pixels lifts
public Macro F1 from $0.41165$ to $0.41826$ ($+0.0066$) and private from $0.38132$ to
$0.40283$ ($+0.022$); the matched-LA public lift is about $+0.012$ ($0.40590\to0.41826$).
The private gain exceeds $3\times$ the public one, suggesting the $336$-pixel pass recovers
rare-species pixels the private split weights heavily. The best public and best private
operating points differ: logit-adjusted single-resolution scores $0.40590$ public but
$0.40600$ private (the project's strongest private result), so we carry both columns
through.

\section{Final Submission}
\label{sec:final_results}

The headline configuration (the i002 four-block checkpoint under the full recipe of
\S\ref{sec:final_submission}) scores \textbf{0.41826} public / $0.40283$ private Macro F1
--- the best public result of the project. Three architecture-orthogonal pivots each rerank
the anchor posterior with an additional signal (Table~\ref{tab:summary}):

\begin{table}[ht]
  \centering
  \scriptsize
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{2pt}
  \begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.55\linewidth}cc@{}}
    \toprule
    Config & Macro F1 & $\Delta$ \\
    \midrule
    BC2.5 last 4 blocks, 224+336+LA, $T{=}0.03$ & \textbf{0.41826} & --- \\
    \hspace{1em}+ cRT triple, $w{=}0.3$ & 0.38195 & $-0.036$ \\
    \hspace{1em}+ Genus $\alpha_g{=}0.1$ & 0.41246 & $-0.006$ \\
    \hspace{1em}+ Pheno., $\beta{=}1.0$ & 0.41346 & $-0.005$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \caption{All four submitted configurations (public Macro F1). The cRT pivot is strongly
  negative; the genus rerank and the phenology prior move the anchor by only $-0.006$ and
  $-0.005$ --- small but consistent in sign. We read this as the BioCLIP~2.5 posterior
  already absorbing both signals through training-distribution co-occurrence
  (\S\ref{sec:orthogonality}).}
  \label{tab:summary}
\end{table}

\textbf{Triple-backbone late fusion (cRT).} This is a feature-level late fusion, not an
architectural pivot. Two MLP heads on a frozen $3328$-d concatenation
of BioCLIP~2.5, DINOv3-Base, and ConvNeXt-V2-L features, mixed with the anchor at $w{=}0.3$,
scored $0.38195$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.036$). A pre-submission gate already flagged the head as
structurally divergent (top-one agreement $0.094$): the divergence was diversity going
\emph{against} signal, a peakier posterior whose confident wrong picks dominate the mix.

\textbf{Genus/family co-occurrence rerank.} A ``siblings stick together'' log-prior built
from the anchor's own aggregated genus/family supports swapped $11\%$ of selections but
moved the score by only $-0.006$ ($0.41246$): a prior derived from the model's own logits
carries essentially no new information.

\textbf{Seasonal phenology prior.} A date-conditioned prior over GBIF day-of-year
histograms, combined multiplicatively with the visual posterior at $\beta{=}1.0$
(Section~\ref{sec:phenology}), flipped $12.8\%$ of top-one picks but scored $0.41346$
($\Delta{=}{-}0.005$). Date is information-theoretically orthogonal to the image, yet the
gain is negligible: the visual model trained on dated images already encodes seasonal
phenotype, and GBIF effort is itself summer-biased. We retain phenology as a novelty
contribution and discuss the orthogonality failure in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
