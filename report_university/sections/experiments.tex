\chapter{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We justify each component of the final system with leaderboard evidence. Many comparisons move more than one factor at once; we flag the confounds where they occur and treat this as a development trace rather than a single controlled ablation.

\section{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}

The task is scored by the Macro F1 of \S\ref{sec:introduction} on a hidden test set of $2{,}105$ vegetation quadrats. Kaggle reports a public score on a fixed subset of quadrats and a private score on the complement; the two do not always rank configurations the same way (\S\ref{sec:infer_ablations}), so we report both wherever a configuration was submitted. Repeated near-identical submissions put the run-to-run noise floor at $\approx 0.002$ Macro F1, and we treat smaller differences as not significant. We also track held-out top-1/top-5 accuracy on a $10\%$ single-plant validation split, but it shows only a weak, statistically non-significant association with quadrat Macro F1 ($r\approx0.4$ over 12 runs; \S\ref{sec:val_lb}), so we use it to choose checkpoints rather than to rank submissions.

\section{Baselines and Supervised Fine-tuning}

Off-the-shelf BioCLIP features are limited: zero-shot tile classification reaches ${\sim}0.12$ public Macro F1, a frozen-backbone linear probe $0.242$, and a few-shot support bank $0.307$. A frozen backbone does not bridge the single-plant to multi-species shift, so we carry only the fine-tuning line forward. Partial fine-tuning of BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 with the taxonomy-aware head produces the main gains: the first strong model (experiment~010: shared trunk, five levels, $1.41$M images) reached $0.391$ public Macro F1, and our final model (experiment~i002: per-head MLPs, three levels, $2.65$M images, 10 epochs) reached $0.412$ public at single-resolution inference, before calibration and ensembling. The comparison changes data, schedule, head topology, and head count at once, so we report i002 as the stronger configuration without isolating a single cause.

\section{Backbone Unfreezing}
\label{sec:blocks_ablation}

The one factor we swept within a single experiment is the number of unfrozen transformer blocks. Within experiment~010 ($1.41$M images) public Macro F1 rises from the head-only baseline ($0.362$) to a peak at eight blocks ($0.391$) and declines at twelve, while the full fine-tune falls to $0.301$ public, losing more of the pretrained representation than the leaderboard rewards. Validation top-5 keeps rising with depth even as the leaderboard score peaks and falls (Figure~\ref{fig:unfreeze_sweep}), the validation/leaderboard decoupling of \S\ref{sec:val_lb}. Our final model (experiment~i002) repeated this on the larger $2.65$M manifest, but the optimum is shallower: public Macro F1 peaks at four blocks (Table~\ref{tab:blocks_i002}), the checkpoint we build the final system on, and the bottom rows show the inference steps that take it to the headline $0.418$.

\section{Tile Aggregation, Calibration, and Ensembling}
\label{sec:infer_ablations}

Of the three pooling rules of \S\ref{sec:adaptive_inference}, softmax-mean performs best: on the four-block i002 checkpoint it exceeds max ($0.412$ against $0.375$ public). \label{sec:tile_aggregation} We then fixed the remaining inference parameters by leaderboard sweep. A joint sweep over $\tau$ and $T$ has a clear optimum at $\tau{=}0.25$, $T{=}0.03$ ($0.406$ public, falling to $0.364$ at $T{=}0.05$ and $0.384$ at $\tau{=}0.5$); at this $\tau$ the adaptive threshold also exceeds fixed top-$k$, with $k{=}2$ the closest ($0.399$). On the four-block checkpoint, logit adjustment moves the operating point from $0.412/0.389$ to $0.406/0.406$, trading roughly $0.006$ public Macro F1 for roughly $0.017$ private, and the $224{+}336$ resolution ensemble then adds about $0.012$ public over the logit-adjusted single-resolution run while leaving private essentially unchanged ($0.406\rightarrow0.403$). Because $\tau$, $T$, the pooling rule, and the resolution pair were all chosen on the public leaderboard rather than on a held-out split, these values are fit to the public test signal; this overfitting partly explains why the public and private rankings disagree, so we treat the absolute numbers with caution and report both columns throughout.

\section{Final Submission}
\label{sec:final_results}

The anchor scores \textbf{0.418} public / $0.403$ private Macro F1, the best public result of the project. Three architecture-orthogonal variants each rerank the anchor posterior with an additional signal, and none improves it (Table~\ref{tab:summary}). The triple-backbone late-fusion variant, a feature-level concatenation of frozen BioCLIP~2.5, DINOv3, and ConvNeXt-V2-L features mixed with the anchor posterior at weight $w{=}0.3$, produced the largest decrease ($\Delta{=}-0.036$); a pre-submission gate had already flagged the head as structurally divergent (top-one agreement $0.094$), so its predictions diverged from the anchor in ways that reduced accuracy. The genus/family co-occurrence rerank, a log-prior built from the anchor's own aggregated supports and mixed in at weight $\alpha_g{=}0.1$, swapped $11\%$ of selections but changed the score only marginally, since a prior derived from the model's own logits carries little new information. The seasonal phenology prior, analysed in Section~\ref{sec:phenology}, was also close to neutral. We interpret this pattern in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
