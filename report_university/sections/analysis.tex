\chapter{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section explains why the system behaves as it does. We first examine the weak relationship between single-plant validation accuracy and quadrat Macro F1, then show that variants within our own backbone converge to nearly identical predictions, and finally report the effect of changing the long tail of the training distribution.

\section{Validation vs Leaderboard Mismatch}
\label{sec:val_lb}

Held-out accuracy on the single-plant distribution appears to predict Macro F1 on the multi-species quadrat leaderboard only weakly. Across our 12 strongest runs the two metrics show a weak association ($r \approx 0.4$) that, with only 12 points, is not statistically significant; within the unfrozen-blocks sweep of \S\ref{sec:blocks_ablation} they nonetheless diverge clearly (Figure~\ref{fig:unfreeze_sweep}), as validation top-5 accuracy rises monotonically with unfrozen capacity while leaderboard Macro F1 peaks at intermediate depth and falls at the full fine-tune. This suggests, though the small sample makes it suggestive rather than conclusive, that the limiting factor is the distribution shift between single-plant and multi-species images rather than model capacity. We therefore use validation accuracy to select checkpoints rather than to rank submissions: the anchor does not coincide with the validation maximum, and the configurations that do (the i003 capped checkpoints and the 015 mix) score lower on the leaderboard.

\section{Within-backbone Saturation and Orthogonality}
\label{sec:orthogonality}

Before turning to architecture-orthogonal evidence, we tested whether additional within-backbone diversity could improve on the i002 anchor by comparing the four- and eight-block i002 checkpoints and their epoch-5 versions on the $2{,}105$ test quadrats. Pairwise top-one agreement is high (up to $0.901$) and a four-way intra-BioCLIP~2.5 ensemble shares a $0.923$ submission Jaccard with the anchor, so the within-backbone variants make almost identical predictions: changing the unfrozen depth or the training-epoch index leaves the prediction distribution largely unchanged. This motivated the three architecture-orthogonal variants (\S\ref{sec:final_results}), on the assumption that any remaining headroom would come from a signal the backbone does not encode.

The small magnitude of the variant deltas (Table~\ref{tab:summary}) points to a property of strongly fine-tuned visual classifiers on biological data: a signal can be independent of the image and still add nothing, because the backbone has already learned it from the training images. The clearest case is the seasonal phenology prior: date is not in the image, yet the prior changed the score only marginally, because the model trained on dated images had already absorbed the seasonal phenotype that date would have supplied. This comparison is, however, confounded: the phenology submission also changed tile screening, the pooling rule, and the tiling (Section~\ref{sec:phenology}), so the net delta is not attributable to the prior alone. With that caveat, the evidence still points away from post-hoc reweighting and toward structurally stronger visual models as the route to further gains (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Long-tail Behaviour}
\label{sec:longtail}

Experiment i003 is the closest the project comes to a controlled manifest comparison, though it is not fully isolated. Relative to i002 it makes two changes at once: it augments the tail with $148{,}063$ GBIF-sourced images for the $2{,}020$ species with fewer than 100 training examples, and it caps every species at 500 images, which flattens the head of the distribution. The net effect was negative, with the i003 best reaching $0.400$ public Macro F1 against $0.418$ for the uncapped i002 checkpoint. Because the two changes act in opposite directions (more tail data, less head data), we cannot attribute the drop to the cap alone; the noisier GBIF tail images may also have hurt. What the comparison does show is that flattening the head while adding lower-quality tail images did not help on this benchmark, which is consistent with high-quality head examples remaining valuable.
