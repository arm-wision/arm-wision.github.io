\chapter{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

This section explains why the system behaves as it does. We first examine the weak relationship between single plant validation accuracy and quadrat Macro F1, then show that variants within our own backbone converge to nearly identical predictions, and finally report the effect of flattening the long tail of the training distribution.

\section{Validation vs Leaderboard Mismatch}
\label{sec:val_lb}

Held out accuracy on the single plant distribution predicts Macro F1 on the multi species quadrat leaderboard only weakly. Across our twelve strongest runs the two metrics correlate at $r \approx 0.4$, and within the unfrozen blocks sweep of \S\ref{sec:blocks_ablation} they diverge (Figure~\ref{fig:unfreeze_sweep}): validation top-5 accuracy rises monotonically with unfrozen capacity while leaderboard Macro F1 peaks at intermediate depth and falls at the full fine tune. This indicates that the limiting factor on this benchmark is the distribution shift between single plant and multi species images, and that model capacity on the single plant distribution is already sufficient. We therefore use validation accuracy to select checkpoints and not to rank submissions: the headline anchor does not coincide with the validation maximum, and the configurations that do (the i003 capped checkpoints and the 015 PlantCLEF24+iNaturalist mix) score lower on the leaderboard.

\section{Within-backbone Saturation and Orthogonality}
\label{sec:orthogonality}

Before turning to architecture orthogonal evidence, we tested whether additional within backbone diversity could improve on the i002 anchor by comparing the four and eight block i002 checkpoints and their epoch-5 versions on the $2{,}105$ test quadrats. Pairwise top one agreement is high (up to $0.901$) and a four way intra-BioCLIP~2.5 ensemble shares a $0.923$ submission Jaccard with the anchor, so the within backbone variants make almost identical predictions: changing the unfrozen depth or the training epoch index leaves the prediction distribution largely unchanged. This motivated the three architecture orthogonal pivots (\S\ref{sec:final_results}), on the assumption that any remaining headroom would come from a signal the backbone does not encode.

The small magnitude of the pivot deltas (Table~\ref{tab:summary}) indicates a property of strongly fine tuned visual classifiers on biological data: information theoretic orthogonality does not imply statistical orthogonality once the model has been trained on data in which the auxiliary signal correlates with image content. The seasonal phenology prior, the most clearly orthogonal source we tested, changed the score only marginally, because the seasonal structure was already implicit in the leaf and flower phenotypes seen during training. Further gains on this benchmark therefore require structurally stronger visual models; post hoc reweighting with auxiliary priors is insufficient (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Long-tail Behaviour}
\label{sec:longtail}

The long tail experiment provides the most controlled comparison in the project. Experiment i003 takes the i002 manifest, augments the tail with $148{,}063$ GBIF sourced images for the $2{,}020$ species with fewer than one hundred training examples, and caps every species at five hundred images, which flattens the head of the distribution. Because i003 changes only the training manifest relative to i002, its leaderboard outcome is directly interpretable: the flatter distribution lowered the score, with the i003 best reaching $0.40041$ public Macro F1 against $0.41826$ for the uncapped i002 checkpoint. On this benchmark the marginal value of additional head class examples is therefore positive when their label quality is high, and capping removes useful information. The augmentation did not lift any species above the hundred image floor, so the cap affected only the head of the distribution.
