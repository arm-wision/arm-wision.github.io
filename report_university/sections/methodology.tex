\chapter{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We describe the final system top-down. It has three parts, a training corpus of single-plant images carrying taxonomic labels, a partial fine-tune of BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 that adapts only the last four transformer blocks and attaches auxiliary taxonomy heads, and a test-time pipeline that tiles each quadrat, pools the per-tile votes, calibrates, and selects an adaptive species set. All leaderboard numbers are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Data and Label Construction}
\label{sec:cap_extra}

The training corpus is the PlantCLEF~2024 single-plant set, each image carrying one species label and its Linnaean ancestry. We re-downloaded it and supplemented it with a research-grade iNaturalist subset, deduplicated the result, and filled genus and family labels for every row, applying no per-species cap (experiment~i001). This yields 2.65 million images across $7{,}806$ species, of which 1.41M come from PlantCLEF~2024 (Pl@ntNet) and cover all $7{,}806$ classes while the remainder come from iNaturalist and cover the $5{,}037$ most populous classes, so the addition strengthens the head of the distribution and does not extend the long tail. A deterministic stratified split holds out $10\%$ per species for validation (seed $42$; species with fewer than five images are kept entirely in training).

\section{BioCLIP 2.5 Fine-tuning and Taxonomy-aware Heads}
\label{sec:backbone}

Contrastive vision-language pretraining organises the embedding space by caption semantics rather than a fixed label set, which suits fine-grained species recognition because morphologically similar taxa sit close together and the space generalises to classes with few labelled images. We use the BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 checkpoint~\cite{bioclip25hf}, from the BioCLIP~2 line~\cite{gu2025bioclip2}, because it is pretrained on TreeOfLife-200M, whose captions encode the full Linnaean hierarchy, so its feature space is already organised by the taxonomy that defines our 7{,}806-class label space and retains structure across the long tail. We fine-tune the BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 encoder partially, keeping the lower 28 transformer blocks frozen and unfreezing only the last four blocks plus the final layer norm and projection. This adapts the encoder to the single-plant distribution while preserving its pretrained representation, and is more robust to optimiser noise on a long-tailed set than full fine-tuning (Section~\ref{sec:blocks_ablation}). Training is two-stage, running 10 epochs head-only with the backbone frozen and then 10 epochs with the last four blocks unfrozen, initialised from the first-stage weights with the optimiser state reset. Both stages use AdamW in bfloat16 with linear warmup and cosine decay (head learning rate $1{\times}10^{-4}$, backbone $1{\times}10^{-6}$, label smoothing $0.1$) on $224{\times}224$ crops with standard augmentation.

On top of the encoder we attach a head that predicts species jointly with coarser taxonomic levels, intended to act as a structural regulariser; we did not isolate this effect with a controlled ablation (\S\ref{sec:discussion}). The final model (experiment~i002) gives each level its own MLP (LayerNorm, Linear $1024{\to}1024$, GELU, Dropout~$0.2$) feeding species ($7{,}806$), genus ($1{,}446$), and family ($181$) classifiers, under a weighted joint cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{species}} \;+\; w_g\,\mathcal{L}_{g} \;+\; w_f\,\mathcal{L}_{f},
\end{equation}
with fixed weights $w_g{=}0.30$, $w_f{=}0.15$ so the coarser levels carry less influence than the species head; missing taxonomy labels are masked out of the loss. An earlier fine-tune (experiment~010) instead used a single shared trunk feeding five taxonomic levels; because the two designs also differ in data, schedule, and unfrozen depth, they are not a controlled ablation.

\section{Tiled Inference, Calibration, and Selection}
\label{sec:adaptive_inference}

The classifier is trained on single plants, but each test quadrat is a dense multi-species scene. We therefore partition each quadrat into a non-overlapping $4{\times}4$ grid of 16 tiles, so most tiles contain only one or a few species, closer to the training distribution. Each tile is forwarded at two resolutions, the native $224$ pixels and $336$ pixels (the ViT-H/14 tolerates the stretch via bicubic positional-embedding interpolation). The $4{\times}4$ grid and the resolution pair are a deliberate but unverified operating point, since a coarser grid down-samples small plants while a finer grid fragments specimens across tile boundaries and multiplies inference cost quadratically. A systematic sweep is left to future work (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

The 16 per-tile predictions are pooled by softmax-mean, where each tile becomes a normalised probability vote and the votes are averaged, so no single tile dominates while evidence still accumulates (we compare against plain mean and max in \S\ref{sec:tile_aggregation}). To counter the long-tailed class frequencies we apply class-prior logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021longtail}, $\ell_s' = \ell_s - \tau\log\tilde{\pi}_s$ with $\tau{=}0.25$, where $\tilde{\pi}_s$ is the Laplace-smoothed training frequency of species $s$, and we average the $224$- and $336$-pixel passes in probability space. Finally we replace top-$k$ selection with an adaptive rule that emits every species whose probability exceeds $T{=}0.03$, subject to a floor $k_{\min}{=}2$ and ceiling $k_{\max}{=}10$ as conservative bounds on the number of species per quadrat.

\section{Final System}
\label{sec:final_submission}

The final system (Figure~\ref{fig:inference_pipeline}) is a single BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14, last four blocks unfrozen, trained under the two-stage schedule on the i001 manifest and run through the tiled inference and calibration of \S\ref{sec:adaptive_inference}. This configuration, which we refer to as the \emph{anchor}, scores $0.418$ public / $0.403$ private Macro~F1. Beyond it we submitted three architecture-orthogonal variants, a triple-backbone late-fusion classifier, a genus/family co-occurrence rerank, and a seasonal phenology prior (Section~\ref{sec:phenology}); none improved over the anchor (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
