\chapter{Method}
\label{sec:method}

We describe the final system top-down. It has three parts: a training corpus of
single-plant images carrying taxonomic labels (\S\ref{sec:cap_extra}); a partial
fine-tune of BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 that adapts only the last four transformer blocks
and attaches auxiliary taxonomy heads (\S\ref{sec:backbone}--\S\ref{sec:bioclip25_010});
and a test-time pipeline that tiles each quadrat, pools the per-tile votes, calibrates,
and selects an adaptive species set (\S\ref{sec:adaptive_inference}--\S\ref{sec:final_submission}).
All leaderboard numbers are deferred to Section~\ref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Data and Label Construction}
\label{sec:cap_extra}

The training data are single-plant images from PlantCLEF~2024, each carrying one
species label and its Linnaean ancestry (genus, family). The distribution is heavily
long-tailed: a few common species exceed one thousand images while hundreds of rare
species carry only a handful. Our final model trains on an enlarged manifest
(experiment~i001): the corpus re-downloaded, supplemented with a research-grade
iNaturalist pull, deduplicated, with genus and family pre-filled for every row, giving
$2.65$\,million images across $7{,}806$ species (Table~\ref{tab:i001_manifest}) and
\emph{no} per-species cap.

\section{BioCLIP~2.5 Fine-tuning}
\label{sec:backbone}

BioCLIP~\cite{stevens2024bioclip} is a contrastive vision-language model whose
TreeOfLife-10M pretraining aligns its feature space with the Linnaean taxonomy. We
fine-tune the BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 encoder \emph{partially}: the lower twenty-eight
transformer blocks stay frozen and only the last four blocks plus the final layer norm
and projection are unfrozen. This adapts the encoder to the single-plant distribution
while preserving the biological prior, and is more robust to optimiser noise on a
long-tailed set than full fine-tuning (the unfreeze-depth ablation that locates this
optimum is in Section~\ref{sec:experiments}). Training is two-stage: ten epochs head-only
with the backbone frozen, then ten epochs with the last four blocks unfrozen, resuming
the weights only. Both stages train in bfloat16 under cross entropy with linear warmup
and cosine decay; the full hyperparameter specification is given in
\S\ref{sec:training_config} (Table~\ref{tab:training_config}).

\section{Taxonomy-aware Heads}
\label{sec:bioclip25_010}

On top of the encoder we attach a head that predicts species jointly with coarser
taxonomic levels, so the auxiliary losses act as a structural regulariser. The final
model (experiment~i002) gives each level its own MLP --- LayerNorm of the $1{,}024$-d CLS
embedding, Linear($1024{\to}1024$), GELU, Dropout~$0.2$ --- feeding species ($7{,}806$),
genus ($1{,}446$), and family ($181$) classifiers. The objective is a weighted joint
cross entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} \;=\; \mathcal{L}_{\text{species}} \;+\; w_g\,\mathcal{L}_{g} \;+\; w_f\,\mathcal{L}_{f},
\end{equation}
with fixed weights $w_g{=}0.30$, $w_f{=}0.15$; the coarser levels are deliberately
down-weighted so the auxiliary heads regularise rather than drive the encoder. Missing
taxonomy labels are encoded as $-1$ and masked out of the loss. An earlier fine-tune
(experiment~010) instead used a single \emph{shared} trunk feeding five taxonomic levels
(Figure~\ref{fig:head_design}); because the two designs also differ in data, schedule and
unfrozen depth, they are not a controlled ablation, and we analyse their feature spaces
only descriptively in \S\ref{sec:head_repr}.

\section{Tiled Inference}
\label{sec:adaptive_inference}

The classifier is trained on single plants, but each test quadrat is a dense,
multi-species scene in which plants overlap and occlude one another. We therefore
partition each quadrat into a non-overlapping $4{\times}4$ grid of sixteen tiles, so most
tiles contain only one or a few species --- closer to the training distribution. Each
tile is forwarded at two resolutions, the native $224$ pixels and $336$ pixels (the
ViT-H/14 tolerates the stretch via bicubic positional-embedding interpolation).

The $4{\times}4$ grid and the $\{224, 336\}$ resolutions are an initial trade-off, not a tuned
optimum. A coarse grid does risk down-sampling small plants --- on a $\sim$2000-pixel quadrat each
$\sim$500-pixel tile is resized to $224$/$336$, so a small seedling can be blurred away --- but a
denser grid multiplies the per-quadrat forward count quadratically ($16\to36\to64$ tiles, each at
two resolutions) and increasingly slices individual plants across tile boundaries, fragmenting a
single specimen's evidence. The $224$/$336$ pair also keeps the inputs near the encoder's $224$-pixel
training resolution while recovering some fine detail. We did not run a controlled sweep over grid
density or tile size on the final checkpoint, for want of submission budget, so we present this
configuration as a deliberate but unverified operating point; a systematic grid-density and tile-size
ablation is left to future work (\S\ref{sec:discussion}).

\section{Tile Aggregation and Selection}
\label{sec:tile_agg_method}

The sixteen per-tile predictions are pooled by \emph{softmax-mean}: each tile is turned
into a normalised probability vote and the votes are averaged, so no single tile can
dominate while evidence still accumulates across tiles (we also tried plain mean and max;
see \S\ref{sec:tile_aggregation}). We then replace top-$k$ selection with an adaptive
rule: emit every species whose probability exceeds a threshold $T{=}0.03$, subject to a
floor $k_{\min}{=}2$ and ceiling $k_{\max}{=}10$, matching the test set's empirical
$\sim$2--3 species per quadrat.

\section{Calibration and Ensembling}
\label{sec:calibration}

Two further steps complete inference. To counter the long-tail prior we apply class-prior
logit adjustment~\cite{menon2021longtail}, $\ell_s' = \ell_s - \tau\log\tilde{\pi}_s$ with
$\tau{=}0.25$, where $\tilde{\pi}_s$ is the Laplace-smoothed training prior. And the
per-tile distributions from the $224$- and $336$-pixel passes are averaged in probability
space before selection --- a single-checkpoint resolution ensemble.

\section{Final System}
\label{sec:final_submission}

The final system is a single BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14, last four blocks unfrozen, trained
under the two-stage schedule on the i001 manifest with the joint species/genus/family
loss. At inference (Figure~\ref{fig:inference_pipeline}) each quadrat is tiled
$4{\times}4$; every tile is encoded at $224$ and $336$ pixels; per-tile softmax
probabilities are averaged across the sixteen tiles and the two resolutions; class-prior
logit adjustment ($\tau{=}0.25$) is applied; and every species above $T{=}0.03$ is
emitted ($k_{\min}{=}2$, $k_{\max}{=}10$). This configuration scores $0.41826$ public /
$0.40283$ private Macro~F1. Beyond it we submitted three architecture-orthogonal pivots
--- a triple-backbone late-fusion classifier (a feature-level concatenation of frozen
BioCLIP~2.5, DINOv3, and ConvNeXt-V2-L features, not an architectural change to any
backbone), a genus/family co-occurrence rerank, and a seasonal
phenology prior (Section~\ref{sec:phenology}); none improved over the visual anchor
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).
