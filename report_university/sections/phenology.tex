\chapter{Seasonal Phenology Prior}
\label{sec:phenology}

The seasonal phenology prior is one of three architecture orthogonal pivots (\S\ref{sec:final_submission}). On the leaderboard it scored just below the visual anchor, and we present it here as a self contained contribution because the date signal it uses is orthogonal to the image. Each test quadrat carries an observation date in its identifier (for example \texttt{CBN-PdlC-A1-20130807}), which the visual backbone does not see. Date is therefore a source of information that is information theoretically orthogonal to the image based posterior, and we use it as a phenological prior combined multiplicatively with the visual posterior in log space:
\begin{equation}
  \log p_{\text{final}}(s \mid I, d) \;=\; \log p_{\text{vis}}(s \mid I) \;+\; \beta \,\log P(s \mid d),
\end{equation}
where $p_{\text{vis}}$ is the BioCLIP 2.5 anchor posterior, $d$ is the day of year, and $\beta$ is a mixing weight ($\beta{=}0$ recovers the visual posterior). The per species observability density $P(s\mid d)$ is estimated nonparametrically from GBIF month of observation histograms, smoothed to a 365 day circular density with a Gaussian kernel ($\sigma{=}18$\,d) and mixed with a small uniform mass ($\epsilon{=}0.05$) to avoid hard zeros. We obtained histograms for $6{,}957$ of $7{,}806$ species ($89.1\%$); the remainder fall back to a uniform density. The submission also screens tiles by an Excess Green vegetation index, pools surviving tiles with an entropy weighted Bayesian mean ($w_t \propto \exp(-H_t)\cdot\mathrm{ExG}_t$), and tiles at two grid scales.

At the natural Bayesian operating point ($\beta{=}1.0$, $T{=}0.03$, $\tau{=}0.25$) the prior changed $12.8\%$ of top one picks but scored $0.41346$ Macro F1 against the $0.41826$ anchor ($\Delta{=}-0.005$). The small effect is consistent with the backbone already encoding seasonal phenotype from its dated training images. The signal is further weakened because GBIF observation effort is itself summer biased, and because the $11\%$ of species that fall back to a uniform density lie in the long tail where the visual model is least certain. We discuss this result further in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
