\chapter{Seasonal Phenology Prior}
\label{sec:phenology}

The seasonal phenology prior is one of three architecture-orthogonal variants (\S\ref{sec:final_submission}). On the leaderboard it scored just below the anchor, and we present it here as a self-contained contribution because the date signal it uses is orthogonal to the image. Each test quadrat carries an observation date in its identifier (for example \texttt{CBN-PdlC-A1-20130807}), which the visual backbone does not see. We use this date as a phenological prior combined multiplicatively with the visual posterior in log space:
\begin{equation}
  \log p_{\text{final}}(s \mid I, d) \;=\; \log p_{\text{vis}}(s \mid I) \;+\; \beta \,\log P(s \mid d),
\end{equation}
where $p_{\text{vis}}$ is the BioCLIP~2.5 anchor posterior, $d$ is the day of year, and $\beta$ is a mixing weight ($\beta{=}0$ recovers the visual posterior). The per-species density $P(s\mid d)$ is estimated nonparametrically from GBIF month-of-observation histograms, smoothed to a 365-day circular density and mixed with a small uniform mass to avoid hard zeros; we obtained histograms for $6{,}957$ of $7{,}806$ species ($89.1\%$), with the remainder falling back to a uniform density. Alongside the prior, this submission also changed three inference components relative to the anchor: an Excess Green tile screen, an entropy-weighted Bayesian pooling rule ($w_t \propto \exp(-H_t)\cdot\mathrm{ExG}_t$), and tiling at two grid scales.

At the natural Bayesian operating point ($\beta{=}1.0$, $T{=}0.03$, $\tau{=}0.25$) the submission changed $12.8\%$ of top-one picks but scored $0.413$ Macro F1 against the $0.418$ anchor ($\Delta{=}-0.005$). Because the tile screening, pooling rule, and tiling changed at the same time as the prior, this net delta is not attributable to the prior alone. With that caveat, the small effect is consistent with the backbone already encoding seasonal phenotype from its dated training images; the signal is further weakened because GBIF observation effort is itself summer-biased, and because the $11\%$ of species that fall back to a uniform density lie in the long tail where the visual model is least certain. We discuss this result further in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
