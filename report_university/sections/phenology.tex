\section{Seasonal Phenology Prior}
\label{sec:phenology}

The seasonal phenology prior is one of three architecture orthogonal pivots (\S\ref{sec:final_submission}). On the leaderboard it scored just below the visual anchor, but the date signal it exploits is genuinely orthogonal to the image, so we present it as a self contained novelty contribution. Each test quadrat carries an observation date in its identifier (for example \texttt{CBN-PdlC-A1-20130807}), which the visual backbone never sees. Date is therefore the cleanest source of information that is information theoretically orthogonal to the image based posterior, and we use it as a phenological prior combined multiplicatively with the visual posterior in log space:
\begin{equation}
  \log p_{\text{final}}(s \mid I, d) \;=\; \log p_{\text{vis}}(s \mid I) \;+\; \beta \,\log P(s \mid d),
\end{equation}
where $p_{\text{vis}}$ is the BioCLIP 2.5 anchor posterior, $d$ is the day of year, and $\beta$ is a mixing weight ($\beta{=}0$ recovers the visual posterior). The per species observability density $P(s\mid d)$ is estimated nonparametrically from GBIF month of observation histograms, smoothed to a 365 day circular density with a Gaussian kernel ($\sigma{=}18$\,d) and mixed with a small uniform mass ($\epsilon{=}0.05$) to avoid hard zeros. We obtained histograms for $6{,}957$ of $7{,}806$ species ($89.1\%$); the remainder fall back to a uniform density. The submission also screens tiles by an Excess Green vegetation index, pools surviving tiles with an entropy weighted Bayesian mean ($w_t \propto \exp(-H_t)\cdot\mathrm{ExG}_t$), and tiles at two grid scales.

At the natural Bayesian operating point ($\beta{=}1.0$, $T{=}0.03$, $\tau{=}0.25$) the prior flipped $12.8\%$ of top one picks but scored $0.41346$ Macro F1 against the $0.41826$ anchor ($\Delta{=}-0.005$). The negligible gain has three plausible causes: GBIF observation effort is itself summer biased, diluting the season signal; the backbone trained on dated images already encodes seasonal phenotype; and the $11\%$ of species that fall back to a uniform density are precisely the long tail where the visual model is most uncertain. We discuss this orthogonality failure further in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
