\chapter{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}

The analysis in Section~\ref{sec:analysis} points to one practical conclusion: real headroom on this benchmark requires structurally stronger visual models, such as end to end fine-tuning that preserves the pretraining prior, multi crop test time augmentation, or synthetic mosaic training distributions, rather than post hoc reweighting with auxiliary priors. At the same time, several limitations qualify what our results establish.

Our strongest model, i002, differs from the earlier 010 fine-tune along several axes at once: the training data ($1.41$M against $2.65$M images from a different manifest), the schedule (five against ten epochs with a larger effective batch), the head topology (a shared MLP trunk against independent per head MLPs), and the head count (five against three taxonomic levels). No run isolates any one of these factors, so we report i002 as the stronger configuration overall and make no causal claim that the head design caused the gain. Nor did we train a species only control without the auxiliary heads, so the auxiliary losses are reported as a deliberate regulariser rather than a measured improvement. The factors we did isolate are the number of unfrozen blocks, which shows a sharp optimum at four (Section~\ref{sec:blocks_ablation}), and the i003 species cap (Section~\ref{sec:longtail}), where only the manifest changed and the score dropped. The head representation study (Section~\ref{sec:head_repr}) is likewise descriptive geometry on single checkpoints rather than a controlled experiment, and our exploration as a whole is a development trace in which most comparisons change several factors at once.

The public and private leaderboards also rank our configurations differently. Our best public submission ($0.41826$) is not our best private one: a lightly logit adjusted single resolution run is strongest on the private split ($0.40600$), and a cross checkpoint ensemble of i002 and 010 is near best private while weak public. Most of the pivot deltas lie within or near the run to run noise floor of roughly $0.002$ characterised in Section~\ref{sec:eval_protocol}, so they should be read as no usable effect rather than as measured decreases. We therefore carry both columns throughout and avoid implying that the best public run is best overall.

Several directions for future work follow directly from these findings. A synthetic mosaic training regime that composes single plant images into multi species scenes with controlled overlap would let the network see the quadrat distribution during training, which no available corpus provides at scale. Self distillation against our production checkpoint, using its predictions on the unlabelled LUCAS pseudo quadrat corpus as confidence filtered soft targets, would exploit unlabelled data in the target domain. A location conditioned bioclimatic prior built from training image coordinates and WorldClim variables would contribute evidence that visual features cannot recover from the image alone, and is blocked here only by the absence of test site coordinates. Finally, the calibration parameters ($\tau{=}0.25$, $T{=}0.03$) and the $4{\times}4$ tiling at $224$ and $336$ pixels were fixed pragmatically under a limited submission budget; per class threshold calibration together with a systematic sweep over grid density and tile size on a held out multi species validation split would establish whether this operating point is optimal or merely adequate.
