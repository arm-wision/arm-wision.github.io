\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

This section justifies each component of the final system (defined top-down in
\S\ref{sec:final_submission}) with leaderboard evidence, in dependency order: the evaluation
protocol, the baseline ladder, the main supervised fine-tuning result, the unfrozen-blocks
ablation, tile aggregation, calibration and ensembling, and finally the submitted
configurations. Throughout, we treat this as a development trace rather than a single
controlled ablation: many comparisons move more than one factor at once, and we flag the
confounds where they occur. The complete per-direction trace, with exploration-depth labels,
is given in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary}.

\subsection{Evaluation Protocol}
\label{sec:eval_protocol}

The PlantCLEF~2026 task is scored by Macro F1 over species sets on a hidden test set of
$2{,}105$ vegetation quadrats. The Kaggle leaderboard reports two numbers: a \emph{public}
Macro F1 computed on a fixed subset of the test quadrats and visible during the competition,
and a \emph{private} Macro F1 computed on the complementary subset and revealed only at the
close. We report both wherever a configuration was submitted, because the two do not always
rank configurations the same way (\S\ref{sec:infer_ablations}); all leaderboard numbers in
this paper are taken from the Kaggle API as archived in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary}.
From repeated near-identical submissions we estimate a run-to-run leaderboard noise floor of
roughly $0.002$ Macro F1, and we treat differences below this as not meaningful.

We also track held-out top-1/top-5 accuracy on a $10\%$ single-plant validation split for
model selection. This validation signal correlates only weakly with quadrat Macro F1
($r\approx0.4$ across our strongest runs); we use it to choose checkpoints but not to rank
final submissions, and we defer the analysis of this decoupling to \S\ref{sec:val_lb}.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}

Before fine-tuning we measured how far an off-the-shelf BioCLIP feature space goes on this
task. Zero-shot tile classification against species text prompts reached only ${\sim}0.12$
public Macro F1; a frozen-backbone linear probe roughly doubled this to $0.242$; a
non-parametric few-shot support bank reached $0.307$; and a single-head partial fine-tune
(experiment 006) reached $0.222$. These baselines establish that BioCLIP carries useful
biological signal but that neither zero-shot matching nor a frozen backbone bridges the
single-plant to multi-species shift. Full per-baseline scores and the remaining early
directions are tabulated in Appendix~\ref{app:experiment_summary}; we carry only the
fine-tuning line forward here.

\subsection{Supervised Fine-tuning Results}
\label{sec:supervised_ft}

The decisive gains came from partially fine-tuning BioCLIP~2.5 ViT-H/14 with the
taxonomy-aware multi-task head of \S\ref{sec:bioclip25_010}. The first strong model,
experiment~010 (shared-trunk head, five taxonomic levels, original $1.41$M-image manifest,
five epochs), reached a best single-configuration public Macro F1 of $0.391$ (private
$0.389$); the four-block checkpoint we use as the reference point for the ablations below
scores $0.38333$. The deployed model, experiment~i002 (per-head MLPs, three taxonomic levels,
the larger $2.65$M-image i001 manifest, ten epochs), reached $0.41165$ public / $0.38132$
private at single-resolution inference, our best single-configuration result before
calibration and ensembling.

We stress that the $010\rightarrow$i002 comparison is \emph{not} a controlled ablation: between
the two we simultaneously changed the training data, the schedule (epochs and effective batch
size), the head topology (shared trunk versus per-head MLPs), and the head count (five versus
three). No run isolates any one of these factors. We therefore report i002 as the stronger
configuration overall and make no causal claim that the per-head head design, or any single
other change, produced the gain; the descriptive head-representation analysis is reported
separately (\S\ref{sec:head_repr}).

\subsection{Backbone Unfreezing}
\label{sec:blocks_ablation}

The one head-adjacent factor we did isolate is the number of unfrozen transformer blocks. We
ran an ablation on the 010 recipe (original manifest, five epochs, identical optimiser and head
configuration), varying only the number of unfrozen blocks. Table~\ref{tab:blocks} reports the
resulting public Macro F1: performance is a sharp optimum, not a plateau. The head-only
baseline corresponds to experiment 006 and the all-blocks (full fine-tune) row to experiment
009.

The lesson is that partial unfreezing is essential but easily overdone: unfreezing the last
four blocks beats both a frozen backbone and deeper unfreezing, and a full fine-tune destroys
the pretrained taxonomy prior. The same four-block setting is used by the deployed i002 anchor.

\subsection{Tile Aggregation}
\label{sec:tile_aggregation}

The three pooling rules that turn the sixteen per-tile predictions into one image-level score
vector are defined in \S\ref{sec:tile_agg_method} and illustrated in
Figure~\ref{fig:aggregation_definitions}: \emph{mean}, \emph{max}, and \emph{softmax-mean}.
Here we report which rule wins on the leaderboard, in the two stages shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:aggregation_results}.

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \input{figures/aggregation_definitions_figure}
  \caption{Inference-time tile-aggregation rules. Aggregation maps the sixteen tile-level species predictions into one image-level score vector. Mean uses all tiles equally and dilutes evidence that is strong in only a few tiles; max keeps each species' single strongest tile and is sensitive to one over-confident tile; softmax-mean averages normalised per-tile probability votes, so evidence accumulates without any one tile dominating. Top-$k$ and probability-threshold selection are applied after aggregation and are not themselves aggregation rules.}
  \label{fig:aggregation_definitions}
\end{figure*}

The first stage is the earlier experiment 010 head-only checkpoint, where we swept all three
rules at a matched tile geometry (\texttt{grid\_4x4}, overlap $0.5$). Because
the checkpoint and grid are held fixed, this is a clean comparison, and plain mean was clearly
the weakest: its best public Macro F1 was $0.25784$ (private $0.32028$) against $0.33830$
(private $0.34039$) for max and $0.34042$ (private $0.37642$) for softmax-mean, roughly $0.08$
public below both alternatives. This matches the dilution argument, so we did not carry plain
mean forward. We treat this as the reason mean was dropped rather than as a result on the final
model, since it comes from an earlier, weaker head-only checkpoint and is confounded by
checkpoint and training-data differences.

The second stage is the deployed i002 four-block checkpoint (\texttt{grid\_4x4}, no overlap,
$224$ pixels), where the submitted comparison was max against softmax-mean only; plain mean was
not re-submitted here, so the 010 mean numbers and the i002 softmax-mean numbers are
\emph{not} a same-checkpoint comparison. Softmax-mean was again the better of the two: at each
method's best selection it reached $0.41165$ public Macro F1 against $0.37509$ for max
($+0.037$), with the same ordering on the private split ($0.38883$ against $0.35724$). We adopt
softmax-mean with an adaptive probability threshold for the final system.

  \centering
  \input{figures/aggregation_results_figure}
  \caption{Two-stage tile-aggregation comparison (public and private Macro F1). The left panel is the earlier experiment 010 head-only checkpoint at matched tile geometry (\texttt{grid\_4x4}, overlap $0.5$), where plain mean was explored and found weakest, so it was dropped. \subsection{Calibration and Ensembling}
\label{sec:infer_ablations}

On the deployed i002 checkpoint we fixed the remaining inference knobs through leaderboard
sweeps: the logit-adjustment temperature $\tau$, the probability threshold $T$, the selection
rule, and the resolution ensemble. Unless noted, numbers are public Macro F1 on the i002
last-four-blocks single-resolution ($224$-pixel) checkpoint.

\textbf{Logit adjustment $\tau$ and probability threshold $T$.} Table~\ref{tab:tau_T} reports
the joint sweep over $\tau \in \{0.25, 0.5\}$ and $T \in \{0.03, 0.05, 0.07\}$. The optimum is
sharp: $\tau{=}0.25$, $T{=}0.03$ dominates every other cell by between $0.022$ and $0.083$ Macro
F1, and Macro F1 falls off monotonically along both axes. Doubling $\tau$ from $0.25$ to $0.50$
at fixed $T{=}0.03$ costs $0.022$, evidence that the long-tail boost saturates quickly and
that pushing it harder starts emitting too many rare-but-implausible species.

\textbf{Selection rule.} Holding $\tau{=}0.25$ fixed, we compared an adaptive probability
threshold (emit every $s$ with $p_s > T$) against fixed top-$k$, a gap rule, and a relative
threshold. Probability-thresholding at $T{=}0.03$ wins decisively (Table~\ref{tab:select_rule});
fixed top-$k$ is second-best at $k{=}2$, consistent with the test set's empirical mean of
${\sim}2$--$3$ species per quadrat. The gap and relative-threshold rules underperform the anchor
by between $0.06$ and $0.10$ Macro F1; their full sweeps are reported in
Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_extra_sweeps}.

\textbf{Resolution ensemble.} Averaging the same checkpoint at two inference resolutions lifts
the result substantially. Going from the best single-resolution $224$-pixel softmax-mean result
before logit adjustment to the $224{+}336$ probability-averaged ensemble at $\tau{=}0.25$,
$T{=}0.03$ raises public Macro F1 from $0.41165$ to $0.418265$ ($\Delta{=}{+}0.0066$) and private
Macro F1 from $0.38132$ to $0.40283$ ($\Delta{=}{+}0.022$). This delta bundles the addition of
logit adjustment with the resolution ensemble; the cleaner matched-LA comparison at $\tau{=}0.25$,
$T{=}0.03$ (single-resolution $0.40590$ from Table~\ref{tab:tau_T} versus ensemble $0.418265$)
gives a public lift of about $+0.012$. The private gain remains more than $3\times$ the public
gain, suggesting that the $336$-pixel pass recovers rare-species pixels the single-resolution
recipe drops, and those species are the ones the private split weights most heavily. This is a
single-checkpoint ensemble of the i002 model, not a multi-checkpoint ensemble.

\textbf{Public/private caveat.} The best public and best private operating points are not the
same submission. Adding logit adjustment to softmax-mean scores $0.40590$ public but $0.40600$
private (the strongest private result of the project), so the configuration we report as the
public headline is not the one that maximises private Macro F1. We carry both columns through the
final-submission table below rather than collapsing to a single number.

\subsection{Final Submission}
\label{sec:final_results}

Our headline configuration is the deployed i002 four-block checkpoint under the full inference
recipe of \S\ref{sec:final_submission}: $4{\times}4$ grid tiling, a $224{+}336$
resolution ensemble, softmax-mean aggregation, logit adjustment ($\tau{=}0.25$), and adaptive
probability-threshold selection ($T{=}0.03$, $k_{\min}{=}2$, $k_{\max}{=}10$). It scores
\textbf{0.418265} public and $0.40283$ private Macro F1, the best public result of the project.

Beyond this anchor we submitted three architecture-orthogonal pivots that each rerank the anchor
posterior with an additional signal; Table~\ref{tab:summary} and Figure~\ref{fig:aggregate_deltas}
collect all four submitted configurations.

\textbf{Pivot 1: triple-backbone classifier retraining (cRT).} We retrained two MLP heads on a
frozen $3328$-d concatenation of BioCLIP~2.5 ($1024$-d), DINOv3-Base ($768$-d), and ConvNeXt-V2-L
($1536$-d) features (best validation accuracy $63.95\%$) and mixed them with the anchor at $w{=}0.3$. A cheap pre-submission diagnostic
gate (top-one agreement and submission Jaccard against the anchor) already flagged the head
as structurally divergent (agreement $0.094$, Jaccard $0.078$). On submission it scored
$\mathbf{0.38195}$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.036$): the divergence was diversity going \emph{against} signal,
a peakier posterior whose high-confidence wrong picks dominate the mix.

\textbf{Pivot 2: genus/family co-occurrence reranking.} A ``siblings stick together'' log-prior
built from the anchor's own aggregated genus/family supports, applied at matched volume
($\alpha_g{=}0.1$, Jaccard $0.887$ with the anchor), swapped $11\%$ of selections but moved the
score by only $\mathbf{0.41246}$ ($\Delta{=}{-}0.006$, inside the noise floor). A prior derived
from the model's own logits carries essentially no new information.

\textbf{Pivot 3: seasonal phenology prior.} A date-conditioned prior over GBIF day-of-year
histograms, combined multiplicatively with the visual posterior at $\beta{=}1.0$ (full method in
Appendix~\ref{sec:phenology}), flipped $12.8\%$ of top-one picks but scored $\mathbf{0.41346}$
($\Delta{=}{-}0.005$, inside the noise floor). Date is information-theoretically orthogonal to the
image, yet the gain is negligible: the visual model trained on dated images already encodes
seasonal phenotype, and the GBIF effort itself is summer-biased. We retain phenology as a novelty
contribution and discuss why it (and the other pivots) fail to add signal in \S\ref{sec:orthogonality}.
